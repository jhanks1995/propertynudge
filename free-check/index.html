This is a simple sense check for private landlords in England, in plain English. It is general information, not legal advice, and it is not a formal inspection. You can change your answers at any time. Nothing is stored unless you choose to email a copy to yourself.

Please choose an answer for every question, then see your result.

1. Do you have a gas safety record from the last 12 months?

If the property has gas, a Gas Safe registered engineer should check every appliance, fitting and flue you provide at least once every 12 months. If there is no gas, this does not apply.

2. Do you have an EICR from the last 5 years?

An Electrical Installation Condition Report should be done by a qualified person at least every 5 years, or sooner if your last report set an earlier date.

3. Do you have an EPC rated E or above?

Most rented homes in England and Wales need a valid Energy Performance Certificate, usually lasting 10 years, and a rating of E or above unless you have a registered exemption. A higher minimum of band C has been proposed, but that is not the law yet.

4. Is there a smoke alarm on every floor, and a carbon monoxide alarm in rooms with a fixed combustion appliance?

You should have a smoke alarm on every floor with living space, and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room used as living space that has a fixed combustion appliance, such as a boiler, fire or wood burner. Gas cookers are the exception. Alarms should be working on the day a tenancy starts.

5. Was the deposit protected, and the prescribed information given, within 30 days?

If you took a deposit, it should go into a government-approved scheme within 30 days, and the tenant should get the scheme's prescribed information in the same 30 days. If you did not take a deposit, this does not apply.

6. Do you have a property licence if your council needs one?

A licence is needed for a large HMO, usually a home rented to 5 or more people from more than one household who share a bathroom or kitchen. Some councils also license smaller HMOs, or all rented homes in a chosen area. Your council can tell you what applies.

7. Have you given your tenant the written information they now need?

For tenancies starting on or after 1 May 2026, you need to give written information about the key terms of the tenancy before it is agreed. For tenancies that started before then, you should have given the Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet by 31 May 2026 if you already had a written agreement. The old How to Rent guide is no longer the document GOV.UK asks landlords to give for these tenancies.

8. Do you know your rental property register date?

The government is rolling out a private rented sector database, sometimes called the landlord or rental property register. Landlords in England will need to register themselves and each property. The rollout is by area from late 2026, and the date for your area should be confirmed on GOV.UK.
