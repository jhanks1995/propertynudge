HMO compliance dates: what landlords need to track

Last checked: 3 November 2026. Applies to England.

A house in multiple occupation (HMO) is a property rented to at least 3 people who aren't one household and who share a kitchen, bathroom or toilet. HMOs carry more rules than a standard let, and most of them come with a date attached.

Do you need a licence?

A licence is mandatory if 5 or more people from 2 or more households live there and share facilities. Councils can also run additional licensing for smaller HMOs, or selective licensing for other lets, so a 3 or 4 person house share may need one too. Rules vary by council, so check yours. When we checked, BCP, Dorset and New Forest councils listed only mandatory HMO licensing, but schemes can be added after consultation.

Licence length and renewal

A licence lasts up to 5 years, and you need one for each HMO. It doesn't roll over, so you must renew before it runs out. Apply well ahead, because councils inspect and consult on a draft licence before granting it. Your licence will also carry conditions, sometimes with their own deadlines for works.

Room sizes and occupancy

Licences granted or renewed since 1 October 2018 must set minimum sleeping room sizes: 6.51 square metres for one person over 10, and 10.22 square metres for two. A room under 4.64 square metres can't be used for sleeping. The licence also sets the maximum number of people per room and in the house.

Management regulations

The Management of HMOs (England) Regulations 2006 apply to every HMO, licensed or not. The manager must:

  • Display their name, address and phone number somewhere prominent in the house.
  • Keep fire escape routes clear and in good repair, and keep fire alarms and fire fighting equipment in good working order.
  • Get the fixed electrics inspected and tested at least every 5 years, and send the certificate to the council within 7 days if asked.
  • Send the council the latest gas safety certificate within 7 days if asked.
  • Keep common parts, water supply and drainage in good order, and provide suitable bins.

Fire safety dates

The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 covers the common parts of most HMOs, so you need a fire risk assessment, kept under regular review and updated after any change. Fire alarm systems and emergency lighting need regular testing and servicing. The intervals depend on the system fitted and the British Standard it follows, so check your licence conditions and your installer's certificate for the exact dates.

Certificates

  • Gas safety check every 12 months. Licensed HMOs usually have to send the council the new certificate each year.
  • EICR at least every 5 years, or sooner if the report says so.
  • Smoke alarm on every storey, and a CO alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance (except gas cookers), checked on the day each tenancy starts.
  • Safety records for any electrical appliances you supply, if the council asks.

Penalties

Running an HMO that needs a licence without one can mean an unlimited fine in court. For offences from 1 May 2026, councils can instead issue a civil penalty of up to £40,000. The same maximum applies to breaching the management regulations.
