EICR: how often do landlords need one, and what happens if you fail?

Last checked: 3 November 2026. Applies to England.

Since 2020 (for new tenancies) and 2021 (for all existing ones), landlords in England must have the electrical installation in a rented home inspected and tested by a qualified person. The report is called an Electrical Installation Condition Report, or EICR.

How often?

At least every five years, or sooner if the last report says so. Some inspectors set a shorter interval, for example three years for an older installation, so check the "next inspection" date on your report, not just the five-year rule.

What do I give people?

  • Existing tenants: a copy within 28 days of the inspection.
  • New tenants: a copy before they move in.
  • Prospective tenants who ask: a copy within 28 days of their request.
  • The local council: a copy within 7 days if they ask.

What do the codes mean?

  • C1: danger present, immediate action.
  • C2: potentially dangerous, urgent action.
  • FI: further investigation needed.
  • C3: improvement recommended, not a fail on its own.

A C1, C2 or FI means the report is "unsatisfactory".

What happens if it fails?

You must get the remedial work (or further investigation) done within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so. Then get written confirmation from a qualified person that the work is done, and send it to the tenant and the council within 28 days of the work.

Fines

Councils can issue civil penalties of up to £30,000 per breach, and can arrange the work themselves and charge you for it.

Tips

  • Book the next EICR a couple of months before it's due. Electricians are busy and remedials can take time.
  • Keep the report and the remedial certificate together.
  • If you rewire or add a circuit, keep the certificate for that work too.
